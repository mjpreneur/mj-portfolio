02 · Email marketing strategy02 / 10

A planned calendar, not random sends

I run email as a revenue channel with a schedule, a segment and a goal behind every send.

Goal
Turn an existing list into repeat revenue without paying for the click again.
Strategy
Plan every campaign on one calendar. Pair one-off launches with automated flows, and resend the winners to the people who opened.
Process
Each launch gets a teaser, a release, a colour or size breakdown and a resend to openers. Shipping news and restocks are segmented by region and by interest.
Result
$676Kin Klaviyo-attributed revenue year to date for one ecommerce brand: $277K from campaigns, $399K from flows.

An always-on flow: back-in-stock alerts

Sold-out products turn into a list of buyers who already said yes. When the item returns, they hear first.

  1. Shopper hits sold outThe product page offers a "Notify when available" button instead of a dead end.
  2. Picks size and channelThey choose their size, then email or text.
  3. Subscribes in secondsOne field, a clear promise, and a privacy line to remove doubt.
  4. Alert on restockThey are told the moment it returns, with urgency: it sold out fast the first time.

04 · Ad creative process04 / 10

Creative built to teach, then move the buyer

I have written, consulted on and edited hundreds of creatives: email graphics, video for info products, UGC images for ecommerce brands. The craft matters less than the reason each one exists.

Goal
Educate the buyer on the product or service, then move them to act.
Strategy
Find where the buyer sits on the awareness ladder and pick the angle that meets them there.
Process
Research, angle, hook, format, test, then scale the winners and retire the rest.
Result
Creative that earns its place through ROAS, with winners scaled in the campaigns shown above.

The five awareness levels

Based on Eugene Schwartz. The same product needs a different message at each step.

ColdReady to buy
01UnawareNo idea there is a problem

Lead with a story or a pattern interrupt that names who they are.

02Problem-awareFeels the pain, no solution

Name the problem in their words, then hint that a fix exists.

03Solution-awareWants the result

Show how this product delivers that result better than the rest.

04Product-awareKnows it, not sure it fits

Proof, reviews, creator content and close-up detail.

05Most awareOnly needs the deal

The offer, the price and the deadline. Nothing else.

06 · Psychology & consumer behaviour06 / 10

Why people buy, and how I use it

This is what I studied and what sits under every ad, email and sales call I build. Each framework is a tool I use to choose a message, not a theory I recite.

AwarenessSchwartz's 5 stages

How much the buyer already knows decides what the message must do.

Where I use itSets the angle and the first line of every ad and email.

DesireWhitman's life forces

Eight built-in desires, from survival and status to social approval, plus nine learned wants such as convenience and bargains.

Where I use itChooses the desire a product speaks to before a word is written.

EmotionGoleman's 8 emotions

Anger, sadness, fear, enjoyment, love, surprise, disgust and shame, each with a family of related feelings.

Where I use itMaps the feeling a hook should trigger, and the one a call should avoid.

BehaviourFogg: B = MAT

Behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a trigger arrive at the same moment.

Where I use itLifts motivation or removes friction: shorter forms, one clear next step.

BiasMunger's 25 tendencies

Reciprocation, social proof, scarcity, contrast, authority and the rest of the ways people decide.

Where I use itSocial proof in creator content, reciprocation in gifting, scarcity on restocks.

SellingRackham's SPIN

Situation, problem, implication, need-payoff. Questions that lead the buyer to the conclusion.

Where I use itStructures discovery on every sales call and every coaching review.

One creative, three levers

The same frameworks, applied to a real sale graphic. Each number marks a decision made on purpose.

  1. 1Scarcity and timing"Pre-Black Friday" tells the buyer there is a window, and that they are early. Munger: scarcity.
  2. 2A price that anchorsThe number is the headline. $4.99 is so low the decision feels free. Behaviour needs ability, so the barrier is removed (Fogg).
  3. 3One clear reward"40% off sitewide" is a single, simple promise, so the next step is obvious and nothing is left to work out.
